# Tarteel: how its mistake tracker works, what people criticise, what we take from it

Research before building a practice mode, 2026-10-04. The app is used mostly around a'maal and
programmes (Kumayl nights, Muharram, Ramadan nights). Tarteel gets daily use from people who
revise memorised Quran with it, and its mistake tracker is the reason. This note covers how
Tarteel works, what its users and critics say, and what that means for a du'a practice mode.

**Sources.** 36 YouTube videos (7.7 h: independent reviews and tests, scholars' rulings, "free
alternative" videos, Tarteel's own tutorials, three co-founder interviews), all transcribed with
large-v3-turbo, in English, Arabic, Urdu and Russian; 1,970 YouTube comments; 12,880 app-store
reviews (Apple, 19 storefronts; Google Play, 9 language/country pulls; 2017 to 2026-10-03);
r/islam posts and threads; Tarteel's blog and support pages; an NVIDIA case study; a systematic
review. Raw data is local only, in `data/research/tarteel/` (gitignored): `transcripts/`,
`comments/`, `reviews/`, `reddit/`, and the scripts in `tools/`.

**Findings in brief**

- **Tarteel's tracker is word-level.** It flags a missed, wrong or extra word and a jump to
  the wrong ayah. Tashkeel checking exists but Tarteel says it "may not catch all". Tajweed is
  not checked. It runs on Tarteel's servers, so it needs internet.
- **They shipped in stages, each one waiting for accuracy.** First voice search, then
  follow-along (over a year before mistake detection), then word mistakes, then letter-level,
  then tashkeel. The founder dropped Google's speech-to-text because "if you're more confident
  that Google made the error than the user, how are you going to tell the user?" A co-founder
  says others failed by starting with tajweed.
- **False alarms are the complaint that costs them paying users.** Pauses, breaths, hesitation,
  stutters, self-corrections and repeated words get flagged. Lag makes it flag words already
  said. It buzzes, then takes the flag back. Users say this "kills concentration", made them
  "lose my self confidence", and makes them "want to quit my tilawa". Some premium users turn
  mistake detection off.
- **Missed mistakes, shown as correct, are the complaint that matters most religiously.** When
  the app doesn't catch a wrong vowel or a near word, it still writes the correct word on the
  screen. A statement attributed to a professor of Quranic sciences calls this "the great
  catastrophe": the reader believes they were right and the error becomes fixed in memory.
- **Spoilers defeat recall.** In hidden mode, saying a common word such as "Allah" can reveal
  the next ayah. Starting mid-surah can reveal what came before it.
- **Paying for correction and crashes are the two biggest complaints.** Each appears in about
  a fifth of 1–3★ reviews (keyword counts). The objection to paying is framed in religious terms
  ("pay to see my own mistakes"), and anger grew where correction moved from free to premium. Opting out of
  recordings used for AI training, which humans review, also needs premium. Women raise this.
- **People value it as a companion.** A recurring positive is "I have nobody to recite
  to". The most-upvoted review of all (986 upvotes) asks for something the du'a app is close
  to: count a dhikr said aloud, hands-free.
- **Tarteel's season is Ramadan too.** A co-founder said in 2021 that Ramadan is their heavy
  season, and the plan was to acquire users then and keep them afterwards. Daily revision with goals,
  streaks and a mistake map is how they keep them.

## 1. How Tarteel works

### What the user sees

| | Free | Premium |
|---|---|---|
| Follow-along | Words light up as you recite; a word that doesn't match isn't written, so you repeat it until it is ([Arabic101](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lz4U7_BS9nU)) | same |
| Memorisation mode | Page hidden; words appear as you recite them | same, plus hints |
| Find where you are | Recite anywhere, it finds the surah and ayah ("Shazam for Quran") | same |
| Mistake detection | no (it was free earlier; reviews from 2022–24 complain it moved) | red word with dotted underline, vibration and a chosen sound ("a clack and a ping to a slap and a ding") |
| Tashkeel | no | yellow; "the app may not catch all of your tashkeel" |
| "Don't progress until it's fixed" | | optional; Tarteel advises new users to start without it, or they get stuck constantly |
| Hints | reveal whole page | peek at the first word of the next ayah (brown), or the whole ayah |
| After a mistake | | "expected X, you said Y", replay your own audio, hear a reciter, mark it "not a mistake" |
| Mistake history | | a map on the mushaf page: red where you err often, fading through yellow and green to nothing as you recite it right; weakest surahs marked; mistakes per page over time |
| Goals | streaks, badges, basic stats | memorisation and review plans ("journey planning"), advanced analytics |
| Recordings | kept, used for AI training with human review | can opt out of AI training |

[one tester](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IjrIzQHXK2c) recited through without a flag), many
letter swaps (ذ for ز, reported in reviews), other riwayat (Hafs only; Warsh is a top request in
North Africa and France).

### How they built it

From the co-founder interviews ([Mohamed Moussa, Jan 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x3fPdclyz6s),
[Moussa with Maruf Yusuf, Apr 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ueDNAJqJxUk),
[Abdellatif Abdelfattah on HazCast, Arabic, Apr 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99Qq-mLDbMo)):

1. **Search.** An Android app (Iqra) used Android's free speech-to-text to find the ayah
   being recited. That was good enough for search, but not for correction: most errors came from
   the recogniser, not the reciter.
2. **Data.** An open-source effort crowdsourced recitations ("#Tarteel10k", 2018). It grew into
   "more than 75,000 minutes of highly curated Quran recitation data" with exact transcripts.
3. **Follow-along** shipped "at least a year, if not more" before mistake detection. They
   improved the fuzzy matching and waited for accuracy.
4. **Word-level mistakes.** "You said the right word or you didn't, or you missed the word or
   you said an extra word… even if it was one letter off… we're going to count it as being
   correct." Next came character-level ("even if these two words sound similar, we're still
   going to… catch it"), then tashkeel ("not yet at the level we'd like").
5. Abdellatif names four phases: words, letters, harakat, tajweed. "The mistake [others] made is
   they started with tajweed, and tajweed is very, very hard to get right from the start."

The models are their own, built with NVIDIA NeMo and Riva. NVIDIA reports a 4% word error rate
and Tarteel "<200 ms latency"
([NVIDIA](https://nvidia.com/en-us/case-studies/automating-real-time-arabic-speech-recognition/),
[Tarteel](https://tarteel.ai/blog/introducing-mistake-detection/)). They published small open
models, e.g. `tarteel-ai/whisper-base-ar-quran`, 5.75% WER on their test set. Users' recordings
feed training: "every time people use Tarteel the data grows and the model gets smarter", and
users are told they get reward twice, for reciting and for improving the model for everyone.

### Business

15M+ downloads; 4.7★ from about 11k ratings on the US App Store. The listed price is from
$4.99 a month; users quote £7.99 a month, $99 a year, €15 a month and a tripled price in Egypt.
Other plans: a family plan (£140 a year for 5 people, from a Reddit post), regional prices
(52,900 NGN a year in Nigeria, 10,000 RUB a year in Russia), premium free during Rajab or
Sha'ban, and an aid programme ("Alim") for people who can't pay. They chose to serve serious
memorisers first, as heavy daily users: "we don't consider Tarteel to be just a Quran app… it's a
memorisation companion." Ramadan is "a very heavy season". At the premium launch (Ramadan
2021, when premium was mainly analytics) they saw about 3,500 sign-ups in two days and 2%
conversion; the plan was to keep Ramadan users afterwards.

## 2. What people criticise

Counts below are rough keyword matches over the 12,880 reviews, for scale only. The quotes are
what matters.

### 2.1 False alarms: the main reason paying users quit

- Flags on pauses, breaths, hesitation, stutters, self-corrections and repeated words: "even if
  you stop for a second it counts it as a mistake"; "flagging mistakes even if I just hesitate
  or stop to take a breath"; "records some stuttering… or repetition as mistake".
- Lag turns into mistakes: "you read a page and it is still on the 3rd line"; Arabic and
  Indonesian reviews report it 3–5 lines behind, then flagging.
- A flag, then the flag taken back: "beeps alerting me, but then corrects itself when it
  realises I said it right… this beep completely kills concentration". One review: "the buzzer
  goes every ayat and rectifies itself".
- After one mistake it "starts to unravel, highlighting more errors that weren't actually made".
- Flags on famous reciters (Sudais, ash-Shatri, Abdul Basit, reported by several users).
  Another user: "I read to a teacher and got 5 mistakes on surat Hajj… on Tarteel 53".
- Voices: "if you're not Arab or have an accent"; a soft voice gives "a sea of red" far from the
  mic and green close to it; an 8-year-old doing hifz.
- What it costs them: "I lost my self confidence"; "gives me anxiety"; "it's making me want to
  quit my tilawa"; "I no longer trust it". A hifz teacher
  ([Daawood Aal Islam](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R8hqMdk0QkM)) knows people who pay for
  premium and turn mistake detection off. Under that video a two-year user says it "got so
  bad… I just quit", and another says the false alarms break khushu'.

### 2.2 Missed mistakes, shown as if correct

- Harakat, letter swaps and near words: "fatha into dhamma… (dha) like (za), it didn't even
  notice"; in free mode "غفور رحيم" for "غفور حليم" goes through (574 upvotes); "mistakes at
  ends of ayat that sound similar are totally missed (huge danger of faulty memorization)"
  (244 upvotes).
- Free mode writes the correct word anyway. You said قوم for قرن and it showed قرن
  ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7b_EIVS3ymg)); you skipped الرسول and it wrote the full
  ayah; "if I repeat the word several times wrongly, it writes it itself" (a comment).
- Starting the wrong ayah: "it will jump to that ayah, rather than telling me I've made a
  mistake".
- The scholarly objection, in a statement attributed to Dr. Abdullah Hashim al-Sharif (Quranic
  sciences, Taibah University) and passed around in comments. I could not verify the original.
  It says the app is unfit for testing memorisation: when the reader makes a clear error (a
  vowel changed, أحد read as أحاد), the correct word still appears, so the reader thinks they
  were right. The error becomes fixed, and they may even defend it on the app's authority. The
  statement says not to use or spread it, and wishes the developers had shown it to scholars
  before release. Sh. Mustafa al-'Adawi, asked whether the app disrespects the Quran, said the
  opposite: "a good thing… a help to righteousness"
  ([1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aX0FwTtTPM), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XKwgtEjr1l8)).
  An Arabic reviewer (189 upvotes) makes the same point as al-Sharif: a reader may get several
  vowels wrong and believe them right because the app didn't flag them.

### 2.3 Spoilers that defeat recall

From [Daawood Aal Islam](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R8hqMdk0QkM): in hidden mode, recite a
common word such as "Allah" and the next ayah can appear. "This makes a person feel like they've
mastered a page when really they've just made the AI hazy enough for them to stumble through."
In reviews: starting from the middle of a surah shows everything before it (Arabic, 51
upvotes). Before peeking existed, the only hint was to uncover the whole page (2021, 83 upvotes).
Others ask for a strict mode that won't continue past a forgotten word (Arabic, 265 upvotes).

### 2.4 Losing the place

Going back to re-read a line or page is often not followed; you must stop the mic and restart
([Arabic review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cKjQQTMfZZQ)). Recording stops after about
30 s of silence without your noticing, so "I have to watch the screen… this scatters my focus".
Similar ayat send you to another surah (Yunus/Luqman, Hadid/Hashr). Repeated ayat in ar-Rahman
and al-Mursalat confuse it.

### 2.5 A mistake history you can't trust or clean

False alarms pollute it ("that page is now covered in red markings"), and early on you couldn't
delete sessions or dismiss flags. A later correction doesn't overwrite the mistake; "listen to
my recitation on each mistake" sometimes plays the wrong clip. Tarteel has since added "not a
mistake", a reset, and marks that fade.

### 2.6 Paying for correction

Paywall and price are about 490 of the 2,337 1–3★ reviews (crashes and bugs about 500).
Representative: "why do we have to pay to see our own mistakes"; "Even Quran is now being used
to generate money"; "instead of being a sadaqah jariyah it may become a sin for you" (Arabic,
118 upvotes); "the free version… is just like a regular Quran app". Requests: a one-time
purchase, ads to unlock, student or regional pricing. Some said they deleted the app when
mistake detection moved to premium, or say they will. The aid programme exists, but reviewers say its form fails
or that they didn't know about it.

### 2.7 Privacy

Recordings are kept and reviewed by humans for training, and opting out needs premium
([support](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/12414504-how-to-opt-out-of-ai-training)). "A
real person somewhere may actually listen to your recitation, which is a big no for most
sisters" (English); "why force me to subscribe when I don't want you to take my voice,
especially as I'm a girl?" (Arabic, 62 upvotes). Others complain they must log in just to read
the Quran, or worry about data-selling scandals in other Muslim apps.

### 2.8 Tone and religious sensitivities

- Gamification: "badges, achievements, 'super reciter'… don't befit an app for the Quran"
  (Arabic, 197 upvotes).
- The start/stop recording sound resembles music (ma'azif); remove it (32 upvotes).
- Marketing overclaims: reviewers call it a "virtual Quran teacher… personal hifz teacher
  24/7". Tarteel's own line is that it does not replace a teacher. Every independent reviewer
  repeats this: learn by talaqqi, use the app for revision.
- Some commenters say deliberately misreciting the Quran, even to test an app, is not allowed.

### 2.9 A tool, not a system

From the hifz teacher's review: no structure, routine or accountability, and weak reminders. He
guesses from experience that "people often download Tarteel for a month or two and then delete
it". A commenter replied that goals and streak notifications exist. Mathani
([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6748412999)) competes on exactly this, with daily
lessons, spaced-repetition review and quizzes ("what comes next?"). A Reddit user wants a quiz
that gives "a word or an ayah… and I have to continue reading from there". The second most
upvoted review of all (Arabic, 705 upvotes, September 2026) asks for a way to write the ayat
from memory and have them corrected: practice that works where you can't recite aloud.

### 2.10 The rest

Crashes and "something went wrong" are nearly as common as price in 1★ reviews (about 170
against 200 of 1,026), and they run through every year. Users also report login failures, Samsung mic issues and needing
internet ("for us with turtle-like internet, it won't function"). Missing mushaf layouts are a
recurring complaint: 16-line Taj, the King Fahd v2 print, the Haramain print, landscape on
iPad. Huffaz remember by page, so the layout matters. Other complaints: no Russian or Turkish
UI; mistakes shown only in Arabic script, so "for those who don't read it, we don't know
where we went wrong" (French).

## 3. What Tarteel gets right

- **Someone to recite to.** "I have nobody to read Quran with… it makes me feel like I've got
  someone"; "like having a companion who listens to my memorisation whenever I want" (Arabic). It
  is the common thread in women's reviews and videos
  ([1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=llhDMTZKorg), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Oroh3bO1T8)).
- **A check before a person.** Huffaz use it "right before I recite to a teacher", and imams
  before leading taraweeh. A child revises with it, then recites to her grandmother
  ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=POMTQ57XoVQ)).
- **Recite from anywhere.** It finds where you are; no menus.
- **The fading mistake map.** It shows where to work next and clears as you improve. Data-minded
  users say this alone justifies premium.
- **Graded hints.** The first word first, then the whole ayah; a peek is logged as a peek, not a
  mistake.
- **"Not a mistake", with your own audio.** You can check the app, and you can see it admit
  errors.
- **Intention in the goal flow.** A goal starts with niyyah and ends with "press and hold to
  commit", framed by the hadith on keeping up with the Quran. That is the respectful version of
  a streak.
- **Answering critics in public.** Tarteel thanked the hifz teacher under his critical video.
  It also gives premium free in Rajab and Sha'ban, and runs an aid programme.

## 4. What this means for a du'a practice mode

The use case. Du'as come in daily and weekly cycles, and the corpus already has them: Du'a Ahd
(after Fajr), the seven weekday du'as, Ziyarat Ashura, Du'a Sabah, the fifteen Munajat, Hadith
Kisa, and the after-salat du'as of Sahifa Fatimiyya. People want to learn these by heart, read
them every day with a check, and prepare before leading a majlis. Children learn short ones for
madrasa. Two of the most-upvoted Tarteel reviews ask for this kind of daily use: voice-counted
dhikr (986 upvotes) and a morning/evening adhkar playlist (361 upvotes).

What to take, in order of how much it would hurt to get wrong:

1. **Precision before recall, measured before building.** Tarteel's founder could not correct
   anyone while the recogniser made more errors than the reciter. Our phone model's character
   error rate on everyday voices is 24.1% (README). Flag a word only on strong evidence. The
   number to own is false flags per 10 minutes of correct reading, on ordinary voices on
   phones, the way `jumps.md` owns jumps per 10 minutes.
2. **Pauses, breaths, repeats, going back and self-corrections are not mistakes.** These are
   Tarteel's main false alarms, and the follower already handles them: stops, line restarts,
   repeats, line backs (`stops.md`, `phone_latency.md`, `out_of_order.md`). This is our head
   start. A practice mode should reuse the follower and flag only what it cannot explain.
3. **Decide once, at a natural point.** No flag that is then taken back. Hold the decision
   until the next words confirm it, or until the end of the line. A late flag is better than one
   that flips.
4. **Never show the reference word as heard when it wasn't.** Use three states: heard,
   not sure, and different (shown only when confident). This answers the scholarly objection
   directly. Say in the UI what is checked: words and lines. Make no claims about vowels or
   tajweed until they are measured, and build in Tarteel's order (words, letters, harakat,
   tajweed).
5. **No spoilers.** In hidden mode, reveal a word only once it is confirmed. Never reveal on a
   lone common word (Allah, ya Rabb, Allahumma, and the refrains). Grade the hints: first
   letter, then first word, then line. Log a hint as a hint.
6. **Quiet feedback during du'a.** No slap, buzz or music-like chime by default: a colour, and
   an optional summary at the end of a line or session. Khushu' comes first. The research on
   learning from tests supports this: delayed feedback costs little or nothing compared with
   immediate feedback, and immediate feedback adds nothing when recall was right
   ([review](https://ict.usc.edu/pubs/The%20costs%20and%20benefits%20of%20providing%20feedback%20during%20learning.pdf)).
7. **Mistake memory the reader controls.** "That was right" on any flag. Marks fade as the line
   is recited correctly. The next session starts with weak lines. Tap a flagged line to hear
   it from a reciter, recite along, then try alone. The timed recordings already exist.
8. **On the phone, no login, no audio out.** The device engine already keeps audio on the phone.
   Make donating recordings opt-in and separate from any paid tier. This answers the privacy
   complaint, especially for women.
9. **Don't charge for being corrected.** If money is ever needed, charge for convenience (sync,
   family or teacher views), not for the check. A waqf or donation model is respected:
   [Maher](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.maher) is free on that basis, and
   Arabic reviewers praise it for it.
10. **Respectful framing.** No XP, badges or "super reciter". Goals carry an intention.
    Encourage reciting to a person. Show it to a scholar before release, and don't demo it by
    misreciting on purpose.
11. **Text you can trust.** Du'a texts differ between books. Show the source, accept known
    variant lines rather than flag them, and show flags on the transliteration too.
12. **A system, gently.** Plans ("Du'a Ahd for 40 days", "learn Kumayl over four weeks"),
    reminders tied to prayer times and days (after Fajr, Thursday night), a "continue from
    this line" quiz, and a silent variant (pick or type the next words) for when reciting
    aloud isn't possible.
13. **Refrains and counts are ours.** Tarteel stumbles on repeated ayat. Du'as are built on
    refrains, and the tracker follows refrain lines 88.6% of the time (README). Counting a
    refrain or dhikr said aloud is a feature people already ask for.

## 5. Before building: what to measure

1. **An error bench on the scenario bench.** Correct readings by bench voices, plus the same
   texts with planted errors: a skipped word (cut out using the alignment's word times), a
   swapped word (synthetic voices reading an edited text), a skipped line, a line from another
   du'a with a shared phrase, a forgotten ending. Non-errors that must stay unflagged: pauses,
   repeats, going back, self-corrections, talk.
2. **Report false flags per 10 minutes on correct reading, catch rate per error type, and time
   to decide.** Write the bars down before the run, as the jump work did. Ship word- and
   line-level checking only if false flags stay near zero on ordinary voices.
3. **Real learners.** A few people learning Du'a Ahd or Ziyarat Ashura, recorded through
   `data/sessions/`. Their real mistakes will differ from planted ones.
4. **Ask before building.** Which daily texts do people want to learn by heart? Do they want
   flags during the du'a or only at the end?
